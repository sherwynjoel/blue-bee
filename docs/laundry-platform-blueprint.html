Cascade — Commercial Laundry & Specialty-Cleaning Operating System

The end-to-end product blueprint for a GTA commercial laundry business: a customer app, an owner app, a driver app and a web console over one backend — built around live vehicle tracking and item-level chain of custody, for the three commercial verticals: carpets/specialty, care & nursing homes, and hotels.

★ What we are building

A vertical operating system for the high-ticket specialty trade and recurring institutional linen — the fundable opportunity identified in the market analysis — not "Uber for consumer laundry." One backend powers four surfaces; the whole system is organized around making every truck and every item locatable in real time.

Strategy carried from the market analysis

Lead with the trade the anchor already supplies. Defer the graveyard.

The market work concluded that "laundry" is four businesses, and the fundable one is the fragmented, high-ticket specialty trade plus recurring B2B linen. This product builds exactly that first, and leaves room (but not MVP scope) for consumer wash-&-fold and laundromat machine-booking later.

  • Phase 1 — anchor: digitize specialty cleaning (rugs, carpets, blinds, upholstery) with live tracking + chain of custody.
  • Phase 2 — expansion: care/nursing homes and hotels — recurring routes, par levels, resident-clothing tracking, cost-center billing.
  • Deferred by design: consumer on-demand (subscription-first, later) and machine-booking (a SaaS wedge). The data model anticipates both.

The product principles

Tracking is the wedge.

Every job and truck is locatable in real time. Transparency beats opaque incumbent contracts and reassures high-value-rug customers.

Chain of custody end to end.

Every item — a silk rug, a resident's cardigan, a hotel duvet — is tagged, scanned, photographed, and accounted for from pickup to return.

One backend, many surfaces.

Customer, owner, dispatcher, plant and driver all read/write the same order and route objects. No parallel spreadsheets.

Vertical-aware, not generic.

A rug is not a linen cart is not a resident's blouse. Three genuinely different workflows, not one forced template.

Offline-tolerant field ops.

Drivers and intake staff work in basements and loading docks. Driver and plant apps work offline and sync.

Honest by construction.

No invented capacity or counts. Liability on high-value items is explicit (inspection + condition photos + signed terms), never hidden.

1 The three commercial verticals

Each vertical is a different customer, a different unit of work, and a different billing model. The platform models them as three distinct workflows sharing one logistics-and-tracking backbone.

Rugs, wall-to-wall carpet, window blinds, upholstery, drapery. Item-centric: each piece is QR-tagged, inspected with condition photos, measured (sq ft), quoted, cleaned through stages, QC'd, and returned with before/after evidence.

3 End-to-end workflows (the lifecycle spine)

Each vertical is a distinct state machine. These are normative — the status enums in the data model and the API transitions must match them exactly. Select a workflow.

Where customers track

At pickup (inbound truck) and delivery (outbound truck) — the green track nodes in the flow. ETA, live map, status timeline, driver card.

Where the owner watches

The fleet map shows every outbound vehicle and flags any late / off-route / failed stop into the exceptions feed in real time.

Where custody is captured

Every scan (intake, stage, pack, pickup, delivery) writes an append-only custody event — the timeline customers and the owner can audit.

4 Live tracking & logistics — the headline capability

The requirement you called out: customers track the inbound pickup vehicle; the owner tracks outbound vehicles; plus scheduling, pickup and drop. Here is both the experience and the architecture.

(a) Customer — "track my truck"

Live map of the assigned vehicle + a dynamic ETA, for both pickup and delivery. Status timeline (Scheduled → Driver assigned → On the way → Arriving → Arrived → Completed) with push/SMS at each step. Driver card (name, photo, masked call + chat). Geofenced "arriving in ~5 min" alert. Shareable read-only link for staff without the app. The customer sees only their one vehicle — never the fleet.

(b) Owner / dispatch — fleet tracking

One map, every active vehicle, colour-coded by status, with live breadcrumbs. Per vehicle: current stop, stops done/remaining, on-time vs plan, next ETA, driver, load. Exceptions surfaced (stalled, off-route, late, failed). Historical breadcrumb replay for disputes, coaching and audits.

Why React Native

One codebase → iOS + Android (your requirement), shared TypeScript with the web/BFF, mature background-geolocation + maps. Flutter is the alternative.

Why a modular monolith

At launch volume it ships faster and stays transactionally consistent. The stateless tracking path peels off first if scale demands.

Forward-compatible

org_id everywhere + Stripe Connect-ready → the same code can become a multi-operator marketplace later, without a rewrite.

7 Build roadmap

Sequenced to the market analysis GTM: prove the anchor, then land institutional linen, then scale, then optionally chase consumer — each phase de-risks the next.

Phase 1~0–4 mo · MVP

Digitize the anchor (specialty / carpets)

Customer app+web (book, quote-approve, live tracking, pay, before/after photos) · Web console (orders, intake/inspection/quote, cleaning stages, QC, routes + live fleet) · Owner app (fleet, today, approvals, exceptions) · Driver app (route, scan, POD, background GPS, offline) · Stripe · notifications · RBAC/audit.

✓ Exit: real specialty GMV flowing with end-to-end tracking + chain of custody
Phase 2~4–10 mo

Institutional linen: care homes + hotels

Contracts / par / standing orders · recurring route auto-generation · soil intake (weight/count) · pack-to-par + load sheets + manifests · resident personal-clothing tracking + lost&found · cost-center billing · B2B portal · batch invoicing + accounting sync.

✓ Exit: recurring contract revenue with proof-of-delivery and per-account loss / on-time scorecards
Phase 3~10–16 mo

Scale & hardening

RFID linen pooling · full production/plant module + rewash/reject/reclaim · BI suite · FR localization · SSO for enterprise accounts · advanced route optimization · uniform/mat-rental adjacency.

✓ Exit: shrinkage controlled, plant fully instrumented, enterprise-ready
Phase 416 mo+

Optional expansion (validate first)

Consumer wash-&-fold (subscription-first, dense core) · laundromat machine-booking SaaS wedge · multi-operator marketplace / multi-city. Each gated on Phase 1–3 traction.

✓ Exit: a repeatable city playbook toward the national-scale vision

9 Risks & open questions

Top risks

  • 🪦 Consumer economics are a graveyard — keep it deferred and subscription-first; don't let "Uber for laundry" creep into MVP.
  • 💎 Damage / loss liability on high-value rugs and resident clothing is the trust make-or-break — mitigated by mandatory intake photos, acknowledged pre-existing damage, and the claims object.
  • 📱 Background GPS reliability across iOS/Android versions (battery, OS throttling) — budget real device-matrix testing; degrade gracefully to last-known + status.
  • 🔄 Offline sync conflicts in plant/driver apps — keep custody/scan events append-only.
  • 👤 Single-anchor concentration — onboard a second specialty supplier early.

Open questions to settle early

  • ❓ "10% of what?" (from the analysis) — net-new only or the anchor's existing volume? Affects billing config, not architecture.
  • 🏥 Resident-clothing liability model — who owns loss risk; drives claim rules.
  • 📡 RFID timing — is Phase 3 soon enough, or does a flagship hotel demand it at signing?
  • 🚛 Own fleet only, or + 3PL/gig overflow? (architecture supports both)
  • 🧾 Accounting system of record (QBO vs Xero) for the anchor → which sync ships first.
  • 🇫🇷 French (Canada) — needed for some institutional RFPs; confirm timing.
